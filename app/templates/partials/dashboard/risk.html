Deadline Proximity Distribution

This chart shows how close your unfinished assignments are to their deadlines, grouped into clear time ranges like “Overdue,” “0–2 days,” or “10+ days.” It helps you quickly see whether most of your workload is safely scheduled in the future or clustered dangerously close to now. Each assignment with a due date is placed into a time bucket based on how many days remain until its deadline. The height of each bar represents how many assignments fall into that category, while tooltips estimate the total amount of work time tied to each bucket.

Assignment Risk Breakdown

This chart breaks down why certain assignments are considered risky by showing the individual factors contributing to their overall risk score. Instead of treating risk as a single number, it reveals how deadline pressure, difficulty, workload overlap, and past performance each play a role. Each bar represents one incomplete assignment, ranked by overall risk. The bar is stacked using weighted components that are normalized to a common scale, allowing you to see which factors dominate and which ones are minor contributors for each assignment.

Risk Composition Evolution

This chart shows how the sources of your academic risk change over time. Rather than focusing on individual assignments, it highlights weekly trends in what is driving your risk — such as rising time pressure, increasing workload overlap, or historically challenging assignment types. Risk components are averaged weekly and combined into a stacked area chart, where each layer represents a different risk factor. The height of the stack reflects total risk contribution, while the relative size of each layer shows how the balance of risk sources evolves week by week.

Urgency Vs Risk Matrix

This matrix visualizes how urgent and how risky your incomplete assignments are at the same time. Assignments in the top-right corner are both time-sensitive and high-risk, making them the most critical to address first. Each bubble represents an assignment, positioned by urgency (how close the deadline is) and overall risk (a weighted combination of difficulty, workload, deadlines, and historical performance). Bubble size reflects estimated work time, helping you balance impact against effort when deciding what to work on next.
