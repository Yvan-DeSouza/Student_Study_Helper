Time Spent vs Expected Time

This chart compares the total time you actually spent studying with the time that was expected for each class. It helps reveal whether certain classes are consistently taking more effort than anticipated, or whether you may be under-investing time in others. Actual time is calculated by summing completed study sessions, while expected time is estimated from assignment types, class types, and your historical study patterns. By comparing the two side by side, the chart highlights over- or under-investment of effort at the class level.

Marginal Returns Curve

This chart shows how your grades change as you invest more cumulative study time. Early increases often reflect strong returns on effort, while flatter sections suggest diminishing returns, where additional study time yields smaller improvements. Each point represents a graded assignment, plotted using the total study time accumulated up to that point and the grade received. The curve is smoothed over time to reduce noise, helping you identify whether studying more generally improves outcomes, plateaus, or becomes inefficient.

Effort Allocation

This chart shows how your total study time is distributed across different classes. It highlights where most of your effort is going and whether that distribution aligns with your priorities or workload expectations. The chart aggregates completed study sessions and calculates the percentage of total study time devoted to each class. This makes it easy to spot imbalances, such as one class consuming a disproportionate share of your effort compared to others.

Outcome Contribution

This chart shows how much each class contributes to your overall academic outcomes. Instead of focusing on effort, it highlights where your grades are actually coming from, helping you see which classes have the greatest impact on your results. Grades from completed assignments are grouped by class and converted into proportional contributions. The chart reflects relative influence, making it easier to compare outcome impact across classes regardless of grading scale or assignment count.
